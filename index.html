Task 2 — Question Types & Structures
Type 1 · Discussion
"Discuss both views and give your opinion"
IntroParaphrase + your position
Body 1View A — the side you agree with less
Body 2View B — the side you lean toward
ConclusionRestate opinion + brief summary
Don't skip discussing one side — that kills Task Achievement.
Type 2 · Opinion
"To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
IntroParaphrase + clear stance
Body 1Reason 1 for your stance
Body 2Reason 2 — or concede opposing view, then counter it
ConclusionRestate stance
Pick a clear side. Sitting on the fence hurts your score.
Type 3 · Problem / Solution
"What are the causes? What are the solutions?"
IntroParaphrase + signal you'll cover both
Body 12 causes with explanation
Body 22 solutions linked to those causes
ConclusionBrief summary
Match solutions to causes — don't introduce new problems in Body 2.
Type 4 · Two-part question
"Why is this happening? Is this positive or negative?"
IntroParaphrase + signal you'll answer both parts
Body 1Answer part 1 fully
Body 2Answer part 2 fully
ConclusionBrief summary
Both parts need roughly equal space — don't neglect one.
Introduction Formula (all 4 types)
1Paraphrase the question — do not copy it word for word.
2State your position or what you will cover. That's it — 2 sentences only.
Never start with: "In this essay I will tell you about..." — it's filler and wastes words.

Some people think that the best way to improve public health is by increasing the number of sports facilities. Others, however, think that this would have little effect on public health and that other measures are required. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Key feedback
  • Task Achievement: ~120 words (min 250). Both views not discussed — jumped straight to personal opinion. "Finally, I take both opinions" is not a conclusion.
  • Grammar: "people will have more aware" and "will have more willing" — systematic errors. Use become more aware / be more willing.
  • Lexical Resource: "obesity or so," "put into reality," "some propaganda" — too informal. Replace with: health issues such as obesity, implemented, public health campaigns.
Band 6.5Draft 2 — after feedback

Nowadays, improving public health has become a major concern for many governments. Some people believe that increasing the number of sports facilities is the best way to encourage a healthier lifestyle. Others, however, argue that this measure alone is not enough and that other actions are needed. This essay will discuss both views and explain why I believe a combination of different measures is more effective.

On the one hand, building more sports facilities can certainly help improve public health. Modern gyms, public swimming pools, football fields, and fitness parks provide people with more opportunities to exercise regularly. When sports facilities are easily accessible and affordable, individuals are more likely to develop healthy habits and stay physically active. For example, many cities that build free outdoor gyms or bicycle lanes often see an increase in the number of people exercising. As a result, problems such as obesity, heart disease, and diabetes may be reduced.

On the other hand, simply increasing sports facilities may have only a limited effect. Many people are aware of the benefits of exercise but still choose inactive lifestyles due to busy work schedules, stress, or lack of motivation. Therefore, other measures are equally important. Governments should promote health education, encourage balanced diets, and reduce the consumption of unhealthy food through higher taxes or stricter regulations. In addition, improving healthcare services and organizing public health campaigns can help people become more conscious of their physical condition and lifestyle choices.

In my opinion, although sports facilities play an important role in promoting exercise, they are not the only solution to public health problems. A healthy society depends on several factors, including education, diet, medical support, and public awareness. Therefore, governments should adopt a comprehensive approach rather than focusing on a single measure.

Key feedback
  • Coherence & Cohesion: Body 2 loses focus — starts as a counterargument but shifts into government recommendations. Keep one job per paragraph.
  • Lexical Resource: "sports facilities" repeated 6 times. Alternate with recreational infrastructure, exercise venues, fitness amenities.

Band 7.0Model answer

While access to sports facilities undoubtedly encourages physical activity, I would argue that a broader set of interventions is required to achieve meaningful improvements in public health.

Proponents of expanding recreational infrastructure contend that greater access directly translates to higher participation rates. When gyms, swimming pools, and public fitness parks are affordable and conveniently located, people are more likely to incorporate exercise into their daily routines. Evidence from several European cities suggests that investment in cycling lanes and outdoor fitness zones has led to measurable increases in physical activity among residents, with associated reductions in obesity and cardiovascular disease.

However, availability alone does not guarantee behavioural change. Many individuals are fully aware of the health benefits of exercise yet remain sedentary due to demanding work schedules, stress, or deeply ingrained habits — a gym that sits unused serves no public health purpose. Addressing these underlying barriers requires complementary measures: public health campaigns that shift attitudes toward preventive care, policies that restrict the marketing of unhealthy food, and workplace initiatives that make active lifestyles more compatible with modern employment. These approaches target the root causes of poor health rather than simply providing infrastructure that only motivated individuals will use.

In my view, sports facilities form one valuable component of a broader public health strategy, but they cannot function effectively in isolation. Governments that combine investment in exercise infrastructure with education, dietary regulation, and community support are far more likely to achieve lasting improvements in population health.

What makes this Band 7.0
  • Body 2 follows claim → reason → example all the way through — no drift into recommendations.
  • Vocabulary is varied and precise: contend, sedentary, ingrained habits, complementary measures, behavioural change.
  • The em-dash in "deeply ingrained habits — a gym that sits unused…" shows grammatical range without being showy.
  • Intro drops "This essay will discuss…" — the position is stated directly, which reads as more confident.
